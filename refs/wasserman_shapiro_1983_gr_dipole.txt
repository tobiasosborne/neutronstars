                                         MASSES, RADII, AND MAGNETIC FIELDS OF PULSATING X-RAY SOURCES:
                                                    IS THE “ STANDARD” MODEL SELF-CONSISTENT?

                                                                        Ira Wasserman1 and Stuart L. Shapiro2
                                                                    Center for Radiophysics and Space Research, Cornell University
                                                                             Received 1982 April 12; accepted 1982 August 5

                                                                               ABSTRACT
                                    We present prototype calculations for Her X-l and 4U 0115 + 63 which are designed to test the
                                  “standard” model of a pulsating X-ray source for self-consistency. We construct “semiempirical”
                                  mass-radius (M-Rs) relations for these two X-ray pulsars based on observational data (luminosities,
                                  pulse periods, spin-up rates, cyclotron spectral features) and their theoretical interpretation in terms
                                  of the MHD accretion torque theory of Ghosh and Lamb. These semiempirical                       relations
                                  depend only on physical processes occurring exterior to an accreting neutron star. We compare them
                                  to theoretical M-Rs relations derived by integrating the hydrostatic equilibrium (Oppenheimer-
                                  Volkoff) equation for representative equations of state which might characterize the condensed
                                  matter in a neutron star’s interior.
                                     Our results support, in general terms, the “ fast rotator,” dipolar, accreting neutron star model for
                                  both Her X-l and 4U 0115 + 63. However, our detailed but preliminary study indicates that
                                  quantitative consistency between our theoretical picture of the interior and exterior of a pulsating
                                  X-ray source may still be lacking. The apparent discrepancy is more pronounced for very stiff
                                  equations of state. We suggest plausible modifications to existing theoretical models which might
                                  remove the discrepancy.
                                  Subject headings: dense matter — equation of state — pulsars — stars: interiors —
                                                    stars: neutron — X-rays: binaries

                                                                                              I. INTRODUCTION
                         Ever since their discovery in the early 1970s, X-ray pulsars have promised to be remote space laboratories for
                      Earth-bound astrophysicists. Theoretical interest in X-ray pulsars stems from the diversity of physical phenomena
                      characteristic of them. Since most X-ray pulsars are beheved to be accreting neutron stars in close binary star systems,
                      their study may lead to new insights on the physics of condensed matter above nuclear densities p > pnuc, pnuc = 2.8 X
                      1014 g cm-3 (see, e.g., Pandharipande, Pines, and Smith 1976; Baym and Pethick 1979; Lamb 1981). Neutron star
                      structure calculations require, in addition to a detailed knowledge of condensed matter physics near nuclear densities,
                      the full strong-field machinery of relativistic gravity. One might therefore hope to learn more about strong-field gravity
                      from observations of X-ray pulsars (see Brecher 1978). The atmospheric science of accreting neutron stars offers the
                      opportunity to study plasma physics and radiation transport at a combination of extremely high temperatures
                      (T« 107-108 K), radiative fluxes (iv« 1024-1026 ergs s-1 cm-2), and magnetic field strengths (Bs « 1012 gauss) that
                      very likely will never be reproduced in terrestrial laboratories (see Zel’dovich and Shakura 1969; Shapiro and Salpeter
                      1975; Basko and Sunyaev 1975; see also Wasserman and Salpeter 1980, and references therein). Farther from the
                      neutron star surface, magnetospheric instabilities arising in strong magnetic and gravitational fields are important in
                      channeling material to the stellar surface (see Arons and Lea 1976, 1980; Eisner and Lamb 1976). Outside the
                      magnetosphere, the two or three-dimensional MHD accretion disk flows that power X-ray pulsars and govern secular
                      variations in their pulse periods pose formidable theoretical problems of wide-ranging astrophysical importance (see
                      Pringle 1981, and references therein).
                         After more than a decade of observations, some of these basic theoretical issues are now addressed by experimental
                      data on X-ray pulsars. Masses have been estimated for six of the known X-ray pulsars (Bahcall 1978; Rappaport and
                      Joss 1981), and the mass of the binary (radio) pulsar has been determined quite accurately (Taylor and Weisberg 1982).
                      These determinations set lower bounds on the maximum mass of stable neutron stars, thereby constraining proposed

                         At present, existing observations of X-ray pulsars provide substantial support for the following “standard” picture.
                      The mass determinations require a moderately stiff equation of state of nuclear matter which allows neutron star
                      masses as high as M > 1.4 M0. The combined data are not inconsistent with the hypothesis that all neutron stars have
                      mass M « 1.4 M0, as might be expected from stellar evolution theory (see Iben 1974; Joss and Rappaport 1976; Arnett

                      extrapolated to the surface, these fields have strengths       « lO11- !!)13 gauss, similar to the surface fields of radio
                      pulsars. Steady accretion at the magnetospheric boundary causes X-ray pulsars to spin up.
                         In this paper we assess the self-consistency of this simple standard model. We do this for the two X-ray pulsars
                      which exhibit cyclotron line features: Her X-l and 4U 0115 + 63. For our prototype self-consistency check, we adopt
                      the accretion torque theory proposed by Ghosh and Lamb (1979) as a concrete example. This theory successfully
                      explains the combined spin-up data for the measured X-ray pulsars. In this theory the main spin-up torque on an X-ray
                      pulsar is due to mass and angular momentum accretion from the inner edge of the disk at r = r0. In addition, there is
                      either a spin-down or a spin-up torque due to the interaction of the pulsar’s dipolar magnetic field with its accretion
                      disk at r > r0. In this theory, given the observed luminosity L, pulse period Ps, and spin-up rate Ps of an X-ray pulsar,
                      one can deduce a relationship between its magnetic dipole moment ji, mass M, and radius Rs. If, as is assumed in the
                      standard model, the neutron star magnetic field is purely dipolar, one can then obtain, entirely from the spin-up data, a
                      prediction for the surface magnetic field Bs in terms of M and Rs.
                         The cyclotron line data may also be used to relate Bs, M, and Rs. Since the interpretation of the cyclotron line data
                      is as yet controversial, we adopt the conservative point of view that the observed features in the spectra of Her X-1 and
                      4U 0115 + 63 are resonant absorption lines at 40 and 20 keV, respectively, formed at the magnetic poles. Upon equating
                      the independent quantities

                      relation with the theoretical M-Rs relations derived by integration of the Oppenheimer-Volkoff (O-V) equation for

                         The two M-Rs curves for each star provide two very different physical perspectives from which to examine an X-ray
                      pulsar. One curve is based on the interior behavior of a neutron star; in particular, it depends on the local equation of
                      state of hadronic matter. The other curve is based on the exterior behavior of a neutron star; it depends on the nature
                      of the plasma accretion flow onto the stellar surface and on the emission properties of plasma just above the surface.
                      The shape of this “exterior” curve is determined by theory, but its normalization must be established by observations.
                      In principle, the requirement that, when combined, the two perspectives yield a definite mass and radius for a given
                      star which agree with independent observational determinations affords a powerful check on the self-consistency of our
                      overall theoretical understanding of X-ray pulsars. In practice, however, the analysis reported here should be regarded
                      as a “prototype calculation” at best, which is limited by the preliminary nature of some of the observational data (e.g.,
                      the cyclotron spectral features) and of the theoretical models (e.g., the equation of state calculations above pnuc, the
                      restriction of the accreting magnetic neutron star calculation to aligned axisymmetric plasma flow, etc.). Hopefully the
                      same analysis will be repeated and prove more useful in future years.

                      qualitative support to the standard model in general and to Ghosh and Lamb’s (1979) “fast rotator” models for
                      Her X-l and 4U 0115 + 63 in particular. However, satisfactory quantitative agreement between existing theory and
                      observations can only be obtained for equations of state of nuclear matter which are not excessively stiff.

                         Given our rather liberal allowances for deviations from the standard model, we tentatively conclude that, if existing
                      accretion torque theory is roughly reliable, quantitative consistency between theory and observation can only be
                      achieved for X-ray pulsars if the equation of state of dense nuclear matter is not very stiff. The data for Her X-l
                      appear to rule out the stiff MF equation of state except under very special circumstances, viz. L~LE and « 1, which
                      seem unlikely. Very soft equations of state with maximum masses greater than 1.4 M0 are not excluded by the data on
                      Her X-1. We feel that a promising equation of state for condensed matter in neutron stars can be constructed by
                      incorporating pion condensation in the TNI nuclear interaction model. Such a calculation has not been performed as
                      yet. We note parenthetically that pion condensation has already been suggested as one possible cure for the
                      discrepancy between “standard” theoretical cooling curves for young neutron stars and Einstein X-ray data for three
                      SN remnants (Cas A, SN 1006, and Tycho; see Nomoto and Tsuruta 1981, and references therein).
                         More significant than our preliminary conclusions is the fact that theoretical models of X-ray pulsars which address
                      entirely different physical processes and characteristics can be tested for self-consistency. X-ray astronomy is on the
                      threshold of making truly significant statements about the physics of highly condensed nuclear matter. We hope that
                      the prototype calculation presented here may prove useful in this regard.
